What Exactly is an Endoscopy?

An endoscopy is a powerful medical procedure that allows a specialist, like a gastroenterologist, to look directly inside your body without performing major surgery. The name comes from two Greek words: "endo," meaning "within," and "skopein," meaning "to see."

A Gentle Look Inside Your Digestive System

The primary tool used is an endoscope—a thin, flexible tube equipped with a high-definition camera and a light source at its tip. This instrument is carefully guided into your digestive tract, transmitting real-time images to a monitor. This allows your doctor to see the lining of your esophagus, stomach, and the first part of your small intestine (the duodenum) with incredible detail.

This direct visualization is far more accurate than many imaging tests for detecting inflammation, ulcers, abnormal growths, and other issues. It’s a minimally invasive way to get definitive answers about what's happening inside your body.

Upper GI Endoscopy vs. Other Types

While "endoscopy" is a broad term, the most common type for stomach-related issues is an Upper GI Endoscopy , also known as an EGD (esophagogastroduodenoscopy). This procedure examines the upper part of your gastrointestinal (GI) tract.

It's important to distinguish it from other procedures. For example, a colonoscopy uses a scope to examine the large intestine and is a critical tool for colon health. You can learn more in our dedicated Patient's Guide to Colonoscopy.


Why Might You Need an Endoscopy? Common Symptoms and Conditions

An endoscopy is both a diagnostic and a therapeutic tool. This means it can be used to find the cause of a problem and, in many cases, treat it during the same session. You might be recommended for an endoscopy if you are experiencing persistent symptoms that need a clear explanation.

Investigating Symptoms: Getting to the Root Cause

An endoscopy is the gold standard for investigating a wide range of digestive symptoms, including:

  • Persistent Heartburn or Acid Reflux (GERD)

  • Difficulty Swallowing (Dysphagia)

  • Chronic Stomach Pain

  • Unexplained Nausea and Vomiting

  • Anemia or signs of internal bleeding

  • Unexplained Weight Loss

Diagnosing Conditions with Precision

During the procedure, the doctor can perform a biopsy, which involves taking a tiny tissue sample for laboratory analysis. This is painless and essential for diagnosing conditions like H. pylori infection, Celiac Disease, and checking for precancerous changes.

Therapeutic Endoscopy: Treating Issues Directly

Modern endoscopy is not just for looking. Dr. Patel can use specialized tools passed through the endoscope to perform treatments on the spot, such as stopping bleeding from ulcers or stretching narrowed areas of the esophagus. Preparing for Your Endoscopy: A Step-by-Step Kaizen Care Checklist

Proper preparation is vital for a safe and successful endoscopy. A clear view of your digestive tract is essential for an accurate diagnosis. At Kaizen Care, we provide detailed, personalized instructions.

The Initial Consultation with Dr. Patel

Your journey begins with a thorough consultation. Dr. Sitendu Kumar Patel will discuss your symptoms, review your medical history, and explain why an endoscopy is recommended. This is your opportunity to ask questions and share any concerns.

Medication and Diet Adjustments

You will need to inform us of all medications you are taking, especially blood thinners, insulin, and iron supplements. We will provide specific instructions on whether to stop or adjust these medications before your procedure.

The Fasting Period: Why It’s Crucial

You will need to have an empty stomach for the procedure. Typically, you will be asked to stop eating and drinking for 6-8 hours before your scheduled appointment. Our team will give you the exact cut-off time.

Arranging for Your Comfort and Care

Because you will receive sedation, your judgment and reflexes will be impaired for the rest of the day. You must arrange for a responsible adult to drive you home and stay with you for a few hours.

The Endoscopy Procedure: What to Expect on the Day in Bilaspur

Knowing the step-by-step process can significantly reduce anxiety. At our Bilaspur clinic, we prioritize your comfort and safety at every stage.

Arrival and Check-in at Kaizen Care

You'll arrive at our center at your scheduled time. Our friendly staff will check you in, and a nurse will review your medical history, confirm you have followed the preparation instructions, and answer any last-minute questions.

Sedation: Ensuring Your Comfort

Most endoscopies are performed under sedation to keep you relaxed and comfortable. This is not general anesthesia. You will be given medication through an IV that makes you feel very drowsy. Most patients sleep through the entire procedure and have little to no memory of it afterward.

The Procedure Itself: A Quick and Precise Examination

Once you are comfortably sedated, Dr. Patel will gently guide the endoscope through your mouth, into your esophagus, stomach, and duodenum. Air is gently used to expand the digestive tract for a better view. The entire process is usually very quick, often lasting only 15 to 30 minutes.

After the Procedure: Recovery and Observation

After the scope is removed, you will be taken to a recovery area to rest while the sedation wears off. Dr. Patel will then speak with you to discuss the initial findings of the procedure.


Is an Endoscopy Painful? Addressing Your Concerns

This is one of the most common worries patients have. The answer is simple: No, an endoscopy should not be painful. Thanks to modern sedation techniques, you will be relaxed and comfortable throughout the procedure. You won't feel the scope moving. The most you might experience is a temporary feeling of bloating or a mild sore throat, which resolves quickly.


Safety and Risks: What You Need to Know

Upper GI endoscopy is a very safe and common procedure. The risks of complications are low but, like any medical procedure, they exist. Choosing a highly experienced gastroenterologist like Dr. Sitendu Kumar Patel, who performs procedures in a state-of-the-art facility, significantly minimizes these risks.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) About Endoscopy

How long does an endoscopy procedure take?

The actual procedure is very quick, typically lasting only 15 to 30 minutes. You should plan to be at the clinic for about 2 to 3 hours in total to account for preparation, the procedure itself, and the recovery period.

Can I drive myself home after an endoscopy?

No. The sedation used during the procedure will impair your coordination and judgment for several hours. For your safety, you must have a responsible adult accompany you and drive you home.

What is the cost of an endoscopy in Bilaspur?

The cost can vary depending on whether a biopsy is needed or if any therapeutic procedures are performed. Our office will provide you with a clear estimate of the costs involved during your consultation.

Will I be fully asleep during the endoscopy?

You will be given sedation that makes you very drowsy and comfortable. Most patients fall asleep and have no memory of the procedure. It is not general anesthesia, so you will be breathing on your own.

How soon will I get my results?

Dr. Patel will discuss his initial visual findings with you immediately after the procedure. If biopsies were taken, the pathology results are typically available within a few days to a week.

What can I eat after my endoscopy?

You can usually resume your normal diet once the effects of the sedation have worn off. We recommend starting with a light meal. Your throat might be a little sore, so it's best to avoid very hot liquids or rough-textured foods for the first day.
